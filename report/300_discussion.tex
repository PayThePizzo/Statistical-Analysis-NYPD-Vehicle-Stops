\chapter{Research Questions and Conclusions}

\paragraph{Are Arrest Outcomes Uniform Across Recorded Stops?}

\textbf{No.} The EDA shows clear heterogeneity across geography, hour and demographics. The Bronx has the highest raw rate in both populations, night and early morning differ strongly from the morning period, Male observations have higher arrest rates than Female observations, and Black and Hispanic categories have higher raw rates than White. The two datasets also differ materially in arrest prevalence and age structure. These patterns are descriptive and do not yet identify independent associations.

\paragraph{Which Disparities Persist After Adjustment?}

\textbf{Several important disparities persist.} Logistic Regression shows that hour, borough, sex, ethnicity and age remain informative after simultaneous adjustment. LASSO then retains the same major signals under shrinkage, while GAM preserves the main sex and ethnicity effects after modelling age and hour more flexibly. The strongest example is ethnicity in four wheeled stops, where Black and Hispanic remain at roughly three times the conditional odds of the White reference across Logistic Regression, LASSO and GAM. Sex is also consistent, with Female conditional odds around $60\%$ lower than Male in the four wheeled models and around $70\%$ lower in the two wheeled models.

\paragraph{Do Two and Four Wheeled Stops Tell the Same Story?}

\textbf{Only partially.} Both datasets reinforce the temporal pattern, lower Female odds and positive Black and Hispanic associations relative to White. However, geography is stronger for two wheels, ethnicity is much stronger for four wheels, and age follows materially different shapes. The comparison therefore supports the original decision to keep the populations separate rather than estimating one common model.

\paragraph{How Predictable Is Arrest?}

\textbf{Moderately as a ranking problem, weakly as an individual classification problem.} All four models perform better than random ranking, but none provides strong separation. The fact that additional flexibility changes AUC only marginally indicates that model form is not the only constraint. The available variables explain only part of the post stop arrest process, which is also consistent with low precision and the modest deviance explained by the GAMs.

\paragraph{Do the Results Establish Excessive or Discriminatory Enforcement?}

\textbf{No causal conclusion can be established from these data alone.} The evidence supports persistent statistical disparities among recorded stops because several raw differences survive multivariable adjustment, shrinkage and alternative modelling assumptions. However, the dataset omits the reason for the stop, detailed legal and behavioural circumstances, motorists who were never stopped and other possible confounders. The analysis can therefore document structured post stop disparities, but cannot determine whether those disparities are caused by discriminatory or excessive enforcement.

\paragraph{Conclusions:} The strongest conclusion supported by the analysis is therefore that \textbf{recorded NYPD vehicle stops exhibit persistent demographic, temporal and geographical disparities in post stop arrest outcomes, with meaningful differences between two wheeled and four wheeled vehicle populations}. The evidence does not establish a clear cause-effect connection behind those disparities and cannot, by itself, prove discriminatory or excessive enforcement. Instead, the analysis identifies where statistical differences remain after the observed context is taken into account and clarifies the limits of what these datasets (and model formulations) can support.
