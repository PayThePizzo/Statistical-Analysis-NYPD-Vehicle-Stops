\chapter{Statistical Modelling and Results}

\paragraph{Modelling Strategy:} Our Model choice follows the same principle of parsimony. Logistic Regression provides an \textbf{interpretable baseline}, \textbf{LASSO tests whether its main signals survive shrinkage}, \textbf{GAM adds controlled non linear flexibility}, and \textbf{Naive Bayes checks the problem from a different probabilistic perspective}. When a tuning objective is required, we often prefer ROC-AUC to likelihood deviance because AUC evaluates ranking over all thresholds and is not dominated by the majority class in the same way as raw classification accuracy. It also avoids privileging one classification direction during tuning. For thresholded evaluation, the operating point is selected on the training data with Youden's criterion, which gives sensitivity and specificity equal weight. This is consistent with the project assumption that missing an arrest and incorrectly labeling a non-arrest are both relevant errors.

\paragraph{Logistic Regression:} The fitted models reproduce the main EDA patterns while clarifying which differences remain after adjustment. \textbf{Time of day is the strongest common signal}. Relative to midnight, the estimated odds at 07:00 are $OR=0.035$ for two wheeled vehicles and $OR=0.160$ for four wheeled vehicles. Thus, conditional arrest odds are approximately $96.5\%$ and $84.0\%$ lower at 07:00 than at midnight, holding the other predictors fixed. The \textbf{important result is the broad morning block of strongly negative coefficients} followed by a progressive return toward the midnight reference in the afternoon and evening. This directly reinforces the raw daily cycle observed in the EDA.

\textbf{Borough also remains relevant}. Using the Bronx as reference, Manhattan North and Manhattan South have odds ratios of approximately $0.332$ and $0.344$ in the two wheeled model. These correspond to about $66.8\%$ and $65.6\%$ lower conditional odds than the Bronx. The geographical differences are smaller for four wheeled vehicles, where Manhattan South and Queens North are approximately $0.554$ and $0.558$. This is consistent with Cramer's $V$.

\textbf{Sex is consistent across the two datasets}. Female observations have $OR=0.300$ for two wheeled vehicles and $OR=0.408$ for four wheeled vehicles relative to Male. In odds terms, this corresponds to approximately $70.0\%$ and $59.2\%$ lower conditional odds of arrest.

\textbf{Reported ethnicity shows the strongest demographic impact}. Relative to White, the two wheeled model estimates $OR_{Black}=1.451$ and $OR_{Hispanic}=1.778$. For example, $OR_{Black}=1.451$ means approximately $45.1\%$ higher estimated odds of arrest than the White reference, conditional on the other included predictors. In the four wheeled model the contrast is much larger, with $OR_{Black}=3.425$ and $OR_{Hispanic}=3.258$. These values correspond to about $3.43$ and $3.26$ times the conditional odds of the White reference category.

\textbf{Age is the main point of disagreement}. In the two wheeled model, Under 18 has $OR=1.725$ relative to the 25--34 reference, approximately $72.5\%$ higher conditional odds. In the four wheeled model the same category has $OR=0.756$, while the older groups decline progressively to $OR_{65+}=0.201$.

\paragraph{LASSO:} The penalty is selected by cross validated ROC-AUC. 
For two wheeled vehicles, $\lambda_{min}=5.3\times10^{-5}$ gives CV-AUC $0.7394$, while $\lambda_{1se}=7.11\times10^{-4}$ gives $0.7373$. The number of non zero coefficients falls from 62 to 53. For four wheeled vehicles, CV-AUC changes only from $0.7524$ to $0.7515$ while the non zero coefficients fall from 63 to 50. The \textbf{1-SE solution, therefore, achieves a useful reduction in complexity with negligible loss of ranking performance}. More importantly, \textbf{shrinkage does not remove the main story}. 

In the two wheeled model, the hour 07:00 odds ratio moves from $0.043$ at $\lambda_{min}$ to $0.141$ at $\lambda_{1se}$, but it \textbf{remains one of the largest coefficients}. \textbf{Black and Hispanic are almost unchanged}, from $1.452$ to $1.473$ and from $1.776$ to $1.780$. Female remains strongly negative and borough also remains relevant.

The four wheeled model behaves similarly. At $\lambda_{1se}$, $OR_{Black}=3.202$, $OR_{Hispanic}=3.057$, $OR_{Female}=0.420$ and $OR_{hour7}=0.228$. Older age groups also retain large negative coefficients. 
The \textbf{important interpretation is that the same temporal and demographic axes remain predictive even when weaker dummy coefficients are aggressively shrunk}.

\paragraph{Generalized Additive Models:} Logistic Regression and LASSO show that hour and age matter, but their categorical representation is not ideal. Hour is cyclic and the EDA suggests a smooth daily pattern, while age behaves differently across ranges and vehicle types. GAMs address this specific limitation without abandoning interpretability.

\paragraph{GAM, Hour as a Non Linear Daily Cycle:} The hour smooth is highly significant in both datasets, with $edf=7.52$ for two wheeled vehicles and $edf=7.925$ for four wheeled vehicles, both with $p<2\times10^{-16}$. Effective degrees of freedom above one provide direct evidence that a simple linear effect would be inadequate. 
More importantly, \textbf{the estimated shape is almost the same in both populations}. Conditional arrest propensity is elevated during late night and very early morning hours, declines sharply from about 04:00, reaches a minimum around 07:00 to 08:00, and then increases through the afternoon and evening. 
The fitted two wheeled smooth spans about $2.3$ log odds units between minimum and maximum, roughly a ten fold change in conditional odds. The four wheeled smooth spans about $1.9$ log odds units, around $6.7$ times in odds. This is the strongest result that consistently survives the EDA, Logistic Regression, LASSO and GAM.

\paragraph{GAM, Age as a Vehicle Specific Effect} Age is also clearly non linear, with $edf=4.66$ for two wheeled vehicles and $edf=4.878$ for four wheeled vehicles, again with $p<2\times10^{-16}$. Unlike hour, however, the shapes differ a lot. For two wheeled vehicles, conditional arrest propensity is relatively elevated among the youngest observations, while for four wheeled vehicles, the fitted effect reaches its maximum around ages 27--30.

The new formulation does not change much in the demographic associations. For two wheeled vehicles, the GAM estimates $OR_{Black}=1.563$, $OR_{Hispanic}=1.900$ and $OR_{Female}=0.309$. For four wheeled vehicles the corresponding values are $3.349$, $3.185$ and $0.403$.

However, the model fit remains limited: the two wheeled GAM explains approximately $9.61\%$ of deviance, while the four wheeled model explains about $9.31\%$.

\paragraph{Naive Bayes:} Naive Bayes provides a different check because it does not rely on the same additive logit formulation. In fact, it models $P(X\mid Y)$ and combines class conditional distributions through Bayes' rule rather than directly fitting $P(Y\mid X)$.

The \textbf{class priors immediately reflect the imbalance}, with $P(Y=1)=0.0538$ for two wheeled vehicles and $0.0325$ for four wheeled vehicles. 

\textbf{Conditional distributions nevertheless reproduce several patterns already observed}. In the two wheeled data, Hispanic motorists constitute about $63.4\%$ of arrests and $46.9\%$ of non arrests, while the Bronx accounts for about $34.9\%$ of arrests and $14.6\%$ of non arrests. In the four wheeled data, Black motorists account for about $47.0\%$ of arrests and $30.0\%$ of non arrests, Hispanic motorists for $37.5\%$ and $26.0\%$, while White motorists show the opposite pattern at about $6.9\%$ and $20.3\%$.

These are class conditional compositions, not arrest probabilities. Their value is that a method based on a very different modelling assumption still identifies ethnicity, time and geography as useful separators. At the same time, the large overlap between the classes explains why its final discrimination remains only moderate.

\paragraph{Predictive Comparison} No model dominates. Four wheeled outcomes are consistently easier to rank, with ROC-AUC around $0.73$ to $0.74$ compared with about $0.68$ to $0.69$ for two wheels. PR-AUC is numerically larger for two wheels because arrest prevalence is also larger. Precision remains low for all models, confirming that these methods are more useful for conditional interpretation than for reliable individual arrest classification.

What we can also say is that, LASSO removes weaker coefficients with almost no loss in AUC, while GAM adds useful non linear interpretation without materially increasing discrimination. Naive Bayes reaches a similar performance range despite its very different assumptions.
